Robotics does not end with a few lines of code. Mechanics, electronics, software, and the mathematics underneath them all have to mesh inside a single robot before it moves. Where do you start showing this wide world to someone who is learning it for the first time? That question is why SooRobotics built RoboCar2 and the textbook together.

## RoboCar2

RoboCar2 is a two-wheeled autonomous robot that SooRobotics developed for robotics education. It is designed so that a student assembles it from parts, reads the sensors, and controls it with ROS 2, experiencing the whole chain on one robot.

- **ESP32-based control** — An ESP32 drives the motors and reads the encoders and sensors, and joins the ROS 2 network through micro-ROS.
- **Sensing** — A time-of-flight distance sensor and an IMU give the robot a sense of its surroundings and its attitude.
- **Pan-tilt camera gimbal** — The camera gimbal on top can be driven from ROS 2 topics or from a smartphone.
- **A body you print yourself** — The chassis and many parts are 3D printed, so students can modify and extend their own robot.
- **Raspberry Pi 5 version in development** — A higher-end version on Ubuntu 24.04 and ROS 2 Jazzy, covering LiDAR SLAM and navigation, is in preparation.

## The textbook: "ROS2 Robot Building 1472"

The course built around RoboCar2 was published as a book in September 2026 by Gumi Seogwan (구미서관). "1472" is read in Korean as *ilsa-cheolli*, an idiom meaning "all at once, without a hitch". The book is structured so that a complete beginner first sees the whole map of robotics, then gets hands-on with the essentials as quickly as possible.

**Contents**

1. Getting started with ESP32-based robot building 1472
2. Smart sensing and the basics of autonomous driving
3. Meeting the robot OS (Docker + ROS 2 + micro-ROS)
4. ROS 2 computer vision and autonomous driving
5. Completing a robot of your own, in hardware
- Appendix: Quick-start guide to a ROS 2 pan-tilt camera gimbal driven from a smartphone

## Student project — a RoboCar carrying a drone

This is a project students carried out with RoboCar2 during the course. They mounted a landing pad on the back of the car to carry a small drone, and drove it by setting a **2D Goal Pose** in RViz on the PC.
